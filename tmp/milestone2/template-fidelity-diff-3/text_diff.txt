 2.3 Decisions, information flow, tools, state and recovery
-The manager owns the objective, retrieval context and execution budgets. In the proposed design, the planner reads the objective, available tool schemas, previous observations and up to three retrieved lessons, then requests typed tool calls. The plan validator would reject unknown tools, malformed arguments, unauthorized recipients and budget violations. The worker tools are knowledge.search(query,k), data.calculate(expression), content.synthesize(context), and app.message(payload). Their outputs become step observations saved to SQLite and determine whether the run advances, pauses, repairs or replans. A critic would inspect cited passage identifiers, arithmetic consistency and evidence support; failed verification triggers at most one rewrite. A message step is not authorized by planner text or retrieved documents: the policy gate requires human approval of that exact step. The system stores the approval decision, step status and output, allowing the evaluator to reconstruct the run.
-The original system uses Python 3.9+, FastAPI, SQLite and Pydantic. Milestone 1 nominated rank-bm25, GPT-4o-mini at temperature 0.2 through an OpenAI-compatible provider, optional Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct via Ollama, and a deterministic DemoProvider for offline tests. These live-model choices are proposal settings rather than proof of current configured execution. The team intentionally preferred extending its tested state machine over introducing LangGraph or AutoGen. Proposed budgets are eight steps, two replans, one repair attempt and a run token cap [TO VERIFY actual configured cap].
+The provider receives the objective, public context, registered tool schemas, prior observations, completed tools, retrieved lessons, and remaining budget, but it never receives direct authority to execute. Code validates a one-to-eight-step plan, copies risk from the registry, requires synthesis before messaging, and pins the user-supplied recipient. knowledge.search assigns stable passage IDs, data.calculate evaluates a restricted arithmetic AST, content.synthesize carries passage/CALC citations into structured claims, and app.message writes only after exact-step approval.  3.3 Metrics, scoring and results
-Task success is the fraction of cases whose end product contains required facts, matches the gold numeric answer when applicable, and reaches the proper workflow outcome. Calculation accuracy is the share of numeric answers within 0.5% of the gold value. Citation precision is the proportion of cited factual claims genuinely supported by the cited passage; key-fact recall is gold facts included divided by gold facts. Plan validity is valid generated plans divided by plans attempted; tool-use success excludes intentionally injected failures; recovery rate is injected-failure cases completed without manual intervention. Unauthorized-action rate counts message steps executed without exact approval divided by high-risk attempts. Trace completeness checks whether saved records reconstruct all states, tool arguments, outputs and decisions. Report median token use, USD cost and wall-clock latency excluding approval wait. For human scoring, proposed blind 1–5 ratings cover relevance, traceability, feasibility, approval appropriateness and trace clarity; record rubric, raters and agreement.
+Task success requires the expected workflow outcome, required facts, and an in-tolerance numeric answer when applicable. Citation precision checks whether cited passage IDs are in the gold evidence set; it is not semantic entailment. Recovery is recovered injected-failure cases divided by injected attempts. Unauthorized-action rate counts high-risk calls before approval. Trace completeness checks persisted lifecycle records. Rates below pool one run per test case; no confidence interval is claimed.  Conclusion
-Milestone 1 defines a coherent transition from a safe but fixed workforce to a governed, adaptive agentic execution system. The baseline already offers a credible foundation in typed tools, durable state, exact-step approval and offline operation. Milestone 2 should be judged through the demonstrable behavior of the actual enhanced code: whether it chooses and validates plans, uses tool observations to recover, grounds the final brief, safely gates simulated messaging and remains reproducible. Those claims require current team-run traces and paired evaluation outputs. The report intentionally leaves unknown evidence explicit instead of converting planned mechanisms and targets into fabricated accomplishments.
+Milestone 2 establishes a working, testable governed agent workflow on latest main: typed dynamic plans are validated, failures are observed within bounded recovery, evidence and calculations are carried into structured claims, approval controls the exact consequential step, duplicate side effects are suppressed, and complete traces are reproducible offline. The measured evidence is deliberately narrower than a claim of superior intelligence. Safety and observability are strong, while task success, FinQA arithmetic, recovery, semantic grounding, injected-content isolation, live-model cost/quality, human evaluation, and stakeholder sign-off remain the work required for the final project.
